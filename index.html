What changes for you.

Concrete shifts, not generic AI promises.

Vocabulary that grows with the communicator.

Pages adapt to the words a person actually reaches for, in the contexts they use them. No quarterly rebuild from an SLP. No starter set that goes stale by month two.

Partner-assisted modeling, in the moment.

Suggested phrases and context-aware page hints help communication partners stay in the conversation, not buried in menus. Designed with SLPs, not at them.

Prediction that respects autonomy.

Suggestions help without putting words in anyone's mouth. The communicator stays the author. The system stays the helper, and stops talking when it should.

Why now.

AAC has not meaningfully changed in fifteen years. Families and SLPs have been asked to live inside apps designed before today's language models existed. LingoLinq starts from current tools and current research, for the people who depend on AAC every day.

Built by someone who lives this.

Hi, I am Scot. I have spent eight years in the AAC industry, and I am the parent of an AAC user. LingoLinq is the app I wished existed when my child started communicating. Open source. Independent. Built with the families and SLPs who use it.
