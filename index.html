What if AAC could meet you where you are?

Language tools have changed more in the past five years than in the twenty before them. We are starting LingoLinq from that simple observation, and looking for people who want to think through what comes next for AAC.

Imagine with us

Three questions worth sitting with.

If you have spent time with AAC, one of these might already feel familiar.

What if vocabulary kept up with the person using it?

What would shift in your week if pages did not need a quarterly rebuild to stay relevant?

What if modeling did not take a workshop?

What conversations would happen, that are not happening now, if the right page were a moment away instead of three menus away?

What if a suggestion helped, instead of taking over?

What would feel different about being the communicator? About being the partner standing next to one?

Why now.

Language tools have changed more in five years than in the twenty before them. AAC has not changed at the same pace. LingoLinq starts from that gap, and from the belief that the people who live with AAC every day should be in the room while it gets rebuilt.

Who is asking.

Hi, I am Scot. I have spent eight years in the AAC industry, and I am the parent of an AAC user. LingoLinq is the project I wished existed when my child started communicating. Open source. Independent. Built alongside the people who use it.

Scot Wahlquist, Founder

What we are not promising.

LingoLinq is pre-launch. We will say plainly what is real and what is roadmap. No certification claims today.

  • Open source. The source and roadmap live on GitHub.
  • Built with the community. Families, SLPs, educators, and AAC users in the loop, not on the sidelines.
  • Privacy and accessibility are inputs to the design, not a marketing layer.
